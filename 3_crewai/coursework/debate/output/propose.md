I strongly support the motion that there should be strict laws to regulate LLMs.

Why? Because LLMs are not harmless tools anymore — they are powerful systems that can influence millions of people at scale, often without transparency, accountability, or meaningful oversight.

First, LLMs can spread misinformation faster and more convincingly than any previous technology. They can generate fake news, impersonate real people, and produce persuasive falsehoods in seconds. In the wrong hands, that means scams, political manipulation, fraud, and social harm on a massive scale.

Second, they pose serious risks to privacy and security. These systems are trained on enormous amounts of data, often including sensitive or copyrighted material. Without strict regulation, companies have little incentive to respect consent, protect personal information, or prevent misuse.

Third, LLMs can reinforce bias and discrimination. If the data they learn from is biased, their outputs can amplify unfair treatment in hiring, lending, education, policing, and healthcare. Leaving this unregulated means embedding inequality into the very systems people increasingly rely on.

Finally, strict laws are necessary because self-regulation has repeatedly failed. When profit is the main motive, safety is too often treated as an afterthought. We do not wait for catastrophic harm before regulating pharmaceuticals, airlines, or financial markets — and we should not wait before regulating LLMs either.

Strict laws do not mean banning innovation. They mean setting clear boundaries: transparency about training data, independent safety testing, liability for harm, and rules against dangerous deployment. That is how we protect the public while still allowing progress.

The choice is simple: either we regulate LLMs now, or we allow unchecked systems to shape society with no accountability. For public safety, fairness, and trust, strict laws are not optional — they are necessary.
